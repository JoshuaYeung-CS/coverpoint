## Killed only by the formal arm (3)

The pre-registration predicted there would be none. All three are wrong reset values
that show at a port only when an APB transfer is in flight as reset is released. Every
test and every random reset in the bench happens with the bus idle, and the coverage
model has no bin for it. The solver is not told when to reset, and finds it in 1 to 3
cycles. (A real APB master shares the reset and would not drive a transfer through it; a
production formal environment might well assume that away, and then these three would be
equivalent under it. They are as much a finding about environment assumptions as about
the design.)

| id | mutation | where | killed by | why it survived |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 314 | `const0` | `cp_uart.sv:241.9-241.17` | formal@3 | `s1` (first synchroniser flop) resets to 0 instead of 1. Looks like a start bit only if `rx_en` is written by a transfer that straddles the release of reset; no test drives the bus during reset. |
| 611 | `inv` | `cp_uart.sv:241.9-241.17` | formal@3 | same register and effect as 314 (`inv` instead of `const0`). |
| 969 | `inv` | `cp_uart.sv:241.9-241.17` | formal@1 | `tx_active` resets to 1 instead of 0 and clears itself one cycle later. `STATUS.tx_busy` is wrong for exactly the first cycle after reset; only a read already in progress as reset is released sees it. |

**no** divisor restriction, to 30 cycles.

| needs 26 to 30 cycles to reach a port | 11 |
| needs more than 30 cycles at any divisor (deep FIFO entries, overruns, long frames) | 78 |

on a serial device, before the sample was drawn.
